Sound familiar?

Nobody handed you the manual.

Somewhere around your twenties, the grown-up part kicked in. Rent. Work. A calendar. And the question “what do I actually want?” got filed under later.

Then somebody asks what your ideal life looks like, and your brain shows you a beach and a laptop. Cute. Not a plan.

It’s not a lack of ambition. Picturing a whole life from a blank page is brutally hard, so most people don’t try. They drift. They wake up in a life that just kind of happened to them.

Drifting is a decision too. It’s just one you didn’t get to make.

The fix isn’t more willpower. It’s a better starting point.

The trick

Stop imagining a whole life. Collect small pieces.

A quiet morning that felt exactly right. A room you didn’t want to leave. An hour that vanished. These are fragments, and they’re real evidence of what fits you. Every page asks for one small piece. The picture builds itself.

Not vibes

Built on real research.

The exercises borrow from well-known work on how people design and regret their lives. Summarized in plain English, with sources listed in the back.

The Odyssey Plan

From Stanford Life Design Lab’s Bill Burnett and Dave Evans: sketch three different five-year versions of your life, instead of hunting for the one perfect answer.

Relationships come first

The Harvard Study of Adult Development has followed people since 1938. Its most durable finding: the quality of close relationships is the strongest predictor of long-term health and happiness.

Regret is a compass

Daniel Pink’s World Regret Survey found the same four core regrets worldwide, and regrets about things not done were roughly twice as common as regrets about things done.

Commutes cost more than minutes

University of Waterloo research linked longer commutes to lower life satisfaction and a stronger sense of time pressure. You’ll price yours in hours.

Studies describe averages. You are the final authority on your own life, and the prototype section is how you find out. This workbook is a reflection tool, not financial, legal, medical, or mental health advice.

Questions

FAQ

What do I need to open it?

Any PDF reader. The free Adobe Acrobat Reader is the safest bet for the fillable fields. The auto-adding totals in Part 8 need Acrobat Reader; if your viewer skips them, you can type the totals yourself.

Can I print it?

Yes. It prints cleanly, so you can fill it in with a pen if you prefer.

I really have no idea what I want. Is it still for me?

That’s the exact person it’s built for. You never have to describe your ideal life from scratch. You just answer small questions about moments, rooms, rhythms and refusals.

How long does it take?

About 6 to 8 hours total, in sessions of 30 to 60 minutes. Do the parts in order and revisit any page freely.

Can I do it again later?

Yes. Keep a blank copy and use “Save as” for each round. Your answers on a life-changing year will look different from your answers on a calm one.

Does it work outside the US?

The exercises are general. Part 8 asks for your own local numbers. The bonus Hotel Dweller figures come from mostly US-based sources, so check them for your area.

Is this therapy or financial advice?

No. It’s a planning and reflection tool. For decisions about your health, money, or legal situation, talk to a qualified professional.
